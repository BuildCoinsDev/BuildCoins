# Welcome To BuildCoinsDev

This community exists because of one question that won't leave me alone:

# Why does it feel like technology is going backwards?

Cars in the 1970s were built to last twenty years. Today they're engineered to fail at sixty thousand miles so you buy another one. Phones from ten years ago felt more solid than what we carry now - I don't even need to explain the Nokia reference. Software that worked perfectly gets "updated" until it breaks. We were promised flying cars, cures for diseases, self-driving vehicles flooding the streets, exponential growth that would change everything. Instead we get a new AI model every six months that is slightly better than the last one, and everyone calls it a revolution.

The speed of real technological progress, the kind that changes how humans live, seems to have quietly stopped. Nobody is talking about why. Because they already know...

The system that funds technology is designed to reward products that keep selling, not products that actually work. A car that lasts forever destroys the car industry. A phone that never breaks destroys the phone industry. Open-Source software that solves your problem completely means you never pay for any solution again. So the people with money to build things have every incentive to build things that fail just slowly enough that you don't notice until you've already paid.

The problem with slow decay is that nobody notices it happening. If your phone died completely on day one you'd return it immediately. But if it slows down just slightly every three months, you adapt. You tell yourself it's fine. Two years later you're carrying a brick and you think you chose to upgrade.

You don't notice your hair growing, one millimeter, every day. But shave it all off at once and the whole room turns around. Real technological regression works exactly like that, invisible in the moment, undeniable in hindsight. We've been losing a millimeter every day for twenty years and only now are people starting to look in the mirror. But after 20 years it's easier to blame it on other factors...

# The problem to be Solved! By this community!

The individuals and small teams who actually want to build something that lasts, they have no money. The organizations with money have no interest in building valuable products.

Nobody has ever successfully built a system that fixes this. Though many have tried throughout history, and that is what BuildCoins is trying to become (with your support).

Every system ever built falls into one of two failure modes.

Governments and large organizations are incredibly stable. They have rules, structures, enforcement mechanisms. Nobody can wake up one morning and unilaterally destroy them. Please! This community is not about politics, some will argue that one or another party made the Government unstable, that's beyond the point I am trying to convey here, stick with me here... The point that I am trying to convey here is that despite being stable, governments are almost completely inefficient. Decisions take years. Bureaucracy kills every good idea before it ships. By the time a large organization responds to a problem, the problem has already mutated three times.

On the other hand individuals and small organizations are incredibly efficient. One person with a clear vision can pivot instantly the entire direction of the full organization, ship in days, change direction the moment reality demands it. But... they are completely unstable. One bad decision, one lost funding round, one burned-out founder, and everything, and I mean completely everything, collapses overnight.

Nobody has ever built a system that has both. A system that can last and run efficiently at the same time.

Not a country. Not a company. Not a DAO. Not a protocol. Every attempt either sacrifices stability or efficiency. That tradeoff is treated as inevitable, as if it's a law of nature. **No! I don't think so.** When I know a problem can be solved, the more people think it's not possible, the more eager I get towards investing time, money and efforts into the project. This is going to be a masterpiece, not because of me but because of the community.

# Failed attempts to solve the problem

This is not a new problem. Humans have been trying to solve it for thousands of years.

The ancient Greeks built democracy, a system designed so no single person could accumulate enough power to corrupt everything. It worked brilliantly for about a generation before wealthy families figured out how to buy votes and the whole thing rotted from the inside.

The Roman Empire built the most sophisticated legal and administrative system the ancient world had ever seen. Roads, aqueducts, governance structures that lasted centuries. Then the incentives shifted, the borders expanded too far, the bureaucracy grew too heavy, and it collapsed under its own weight.

Socialism and communism are perhaps the most discussed examples, socialism says the state owns the means of production and distributes wealth collectively, communism takes it further and says the state itself eventually dissolves into a classless society where everyone contributes and takes only what they need.

When the only measure of success is money, everything that cannot be measured in money gets destroyed. Community. Quality. Long-term thinking. The environment. The health of the people doing the work.

However, the flaw was that human beings, on average, need pressure to perform. Not because they are lazy by nature, but because comfort is the natural state every organism seeks. Remove the consequence of not working and most people, will eventually choose comfort over contribution. The economy does not move. The shelves go empty. The innovation stops. This is not a moral judgment. It is biology. Every organism conserves energy when survival is not at stake.

Capitalism understood this brutally well. Make survival conditional on contribution and people work. Hard. Brilliantly. Desperately. The engine runs. Wealth gets created at a scale no other system in history has matched. Capitalism lifted more people out of poverty in two hundred years than every other system combined across all of recorded history. That is not propaganda. That is data.

But capitalism has a flaw that was always there and is now impossible to ignore.

The same survival pressure that makes people work hard also makes them cheat since cheating becomes more profitable than hard-work. And the more capital you accumulate, the easier cheating becomes. A small business owner cannot afford to build inferior products, their reputation is their survival. A startup has to innovate to eat. A company that controls seventy percent of a market can buy every startup before it threatens them and never innovate again.

Capitalism was designed for a world where competition was real and unavoidable. It is now operating in a world where enough money can eliminate competition entirely. The engine is still running. But it stopped creating and started extracting. That is not capitalism working. That is capitalism being abused by its own logic until it undermines itself.

# A coin has more than two sides. If you look closely, the edge is actually a circle with infinitely many sides, all telling the same story from a different angle.

Every system in history has eventually been reverse-engineered by the humans inside it. Not because humans are evil. Because humans are human. Clever, adaptive, self-interested, and relentlessly creative about finding the path of least resistance.

In 1965, the average CEO of a large American company earned 21 times more than a typical worker. By 2024, that ratio was 281 to 1. At the top 100 S&P 500 companies it reached 603 to 1. Between 2022 and 2024 alone, over 460,000 tech workers were laid off. In 2025, another 166,000 followed. When Meta laid off 21,000 employees and announced the results, their stock rose $75 in a single day, adding $196 billion to their market capitalization overnight. A Wall Street record.

The remaining workers don't just absorb the emptied desks. They absorb the workload. More hours, more pressure, fewer people, same deadlines, except now AI is supposedly doing everything, so why are you complaining? Right?

The products built under these conditions reflect it. Security breaches are accelerating. Critical infrastructure is understaffed. The systems that the entire global economy runs on are being maintained by exhausted people at companies optimizing for next quarter's stock price. We are not in a period of technological advancement. We are in a period of technological extraction, harvesting the value built by previous generations while investing as little as possible in what comes next.

# And somewhere in a boardroom, someone is calling this efficiency.

No single person should have the power to make every decision. But we also don't need a thousand signatures before a new GitHub repository can be created. Every system in history collapsed into one of those two extremes, one person with too much power, or a committee so large nothing ever moves. The middle ground has never been successfully built. That is what this community exists to find. Real design, forming a structure we will trust today, tomorrow, and in twenty years from now. Something incorruptible not because we believe in the goodness of people, but because the system makes corruption structurally impossible.

Gitcoin distributed sixty million dollars to support open-source project's developers. But they never controlled the direction of the project. They never made external profits since those open-source projects were, well, open-source project. It didn't scale. Build Finance DAO tried to go further, a decentralized venture builder. Impressive! A company with no boss, no office, no headquarters. Just a shared wallet full of money and a rule: anyone who owns enough voting tokens gets to decide what the money does. Until one attacker drained the entire treasury in a single transaction. About 470,000$. Gone overnight.

How do you build a system that is efficient enough to ship real products, incorruptible enough that no insider, no bot, no rogue actor, no government, no single person with a knife to the founder's throat can destroy it, but still controlled enough that it cannot be hijacked and pointed in the wrong direction? A system that builds software that lasts, not software that sells. That changes something real, once and for all.

I am going to offer a $1,000 challenge. Real money for whoever designs the best governance system that solves the stability-efficiency paradox. Every submission public. Every critique public. The community evaluates together.
